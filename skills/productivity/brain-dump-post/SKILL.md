# Brain-dump post

A brain dump is a low-stakes channel where coworkers lurk: a curated handful of things worth clicking, in the author's plain voice. Readers scan, click one or two links, and decide whether the channel is worth watching. Every link spends a little of the author's credibility, so a dud costs more than a gap.

Input is a corpus from [bookmark-corpus](../bookmark-corpus/SKILL.md). When none exists for the week, run that skill first or ask for one.

## 1. Select

Pick each item for what a coworker gets when they click it. Keep an item only when it passes all six:

1. **Payoff:** the click gives something in a couple of minutes: a writeup, repo or working demo.
2. **Detail:** it has one fact someone would repeat at lunch, writable in a line.
3. **Changes something:** it surprises, or is usable today.
4. **Reason to care:** it touches the readers' work or field, or is simply a pleasure.
5. **Light caveats:** the caveat is shorter than the pitch. For hyped things, the counterpoint is the valuable line; include it.
6. **Fresh:** no `repeat` on the card, unless something genuinely new happened.

Then shape the set:

- Popularity is attention, not importance: a 60-bookmark cluster gets 1–3 slots, usually its best application and its counterpoint.
- About 3 items per topic, 12–15 in total. Leave good things out.
- Mix effort: something usable today, something to read this weekend, something to think about, and at least one non-work item.

**Done:** every chosen item passes the six tests, and you can name in a few words why each strong candidate you cut was cut.

## 2. Write

The voice is a person typing in Slack. [references/example.md](references/example.md) is an approved post; match it.

- lowercase, plain sentences. Open with `brain dump for the week` and go straight into the first item.
- Group with plain topic words and a colon (`testing:`, `design:`, `reading:`), then `-` bullets. A lone item can stand ungrouped.
- Put the link text on the thing itself: `steipete [deleted ~400k lines of tests](…)`.
- Each bullet says what the thing is, then its detail. Length follows the item, so bullets vary.
- A counterpoint gets its own bullet or clause in the same plain voice.
- End on one line, often a question worth leaving people with.
- Formatting stays plain: no emoji headers, bold taglines, clever section names, thesis opener or lesson after each group. Drafts with those read as AI-written and kitschy.
- Opinions in the author's voice stay mild and generic ("worth reading"). Their experiences and endorsements appear only when they supplied them.

**Done:** every bullet has a link and a concrete detail, and reads without the corpus.

## 3. Check and deliver

- Read the post as a coworker scrolling Slack and cut what you would skip.
- Check each detail against its card, conditions included.
- Return the post, then brief notes: strong candidates you cut and why, every first-person judgment for the author to confirm, and any repeats you kept.
- Save the final post as `brain-dump-<end-date>.md` beside the corpus, so next week's repeat check can find it.
- Deliver a draft. Posting happens only when the author asks.
